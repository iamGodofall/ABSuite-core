# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be straight with people. Argue with the work, not the person. Assume the other
party is competent and acting in good faith until they show you otherwise.

## What is expected

- **Disagree specifically.** "This is wrong because X returns Y when Z" is
  useful. "This is bad" is not, and neither is agreeing to avoid friction.
- **Say when you are unsure.** A stated assumption is worth more than a
  confident guess, and this project holds its own documentation to that.
- **Take correction plainly.** Being wrong in public is the ordinary cost of
  working in public. Fix it and carry on; nobody needs the apology.
- **Credit people.** Including the person who found the bug you fixed.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, personal attacks, or demeaning comments about someone's identity,
  background, or level of experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately derailing discussions, or arguing in bad faith to exhaust the
  other party rather than to reach an answer.
- Sustained hostility dressed up as bluntness. Directness is welcome; contempt
  is not, and the difference is usually obvious to everyone watching.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review, and
anywhere someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Email **info@socialfeed.co.za**. Reports are read by the maintainer and nobody
else.

Tell us what happened and where. You do not need to prove anything to a standard
before reporting it — a vague report of something real is worth more than
silence, and you will not be asked to confront the other person.

This project is maintained by one person, which is worth saying plainly: there
is no committee, no appeals board, and a conflict involving the maintainer has
no neutral party inside the project to hear it. If that is your situation,
saying so publicly is legitimate and will not be held against you.

## Enforcement

What happens depends on what occurred:

| | |
|---|---|
| A first, minor lapse | A private note explaining what was wrong |
| Repeated, or a clear violation | A public correction and a request to stop |
| Sustained or severe | Blocked from the repository |

Anything involving harassment or someone's safety skips straight to the last
row. Nobody is owed a warning before being removed for that.
